Sources

Every figure quoted on the About page and used to calibrate this dashboard's fallback estimates is cited below, alongside the sources behind our name.

Environmental Footprint of AI

Carbon Emissions

  • Patterson, Gonzalez, Le, Liang, Munguia, Rothchild, So, Texier, Dean - "Carbon Emissions and Large Neural Network Training" (2021) arXiv:2104.10350 - full training-footprint methodology; estimates GPT-3's training at ~552 tCO2eq.
  • Luccioni, Jernite, Strubell - "Power Hungry Processing: Watts Driving the Cost of AI Deployment?" (2024, ACM FAccT) arXiv:2311.16863 - first systematic comparison of inference-time energy/carbon cost across model types.
  • Jegham, Abdelatti, Elmoubarki, Hendawi - "How Hungry is AI? Benchmarking Energy, Water, and Carbon Footprint of LLM Inference" (2025) arXiv:2505.09598 - infrastructure-aware benchmark of 30 commercial models, with energy, water, and carbon side by side.
  • "CarbonScaling: Extending Neural Scaling Laws for Carbon Footprint in LLMs" (2025) arXiv:2508.06524 - a scaling-law approach for estimating carbon footprint from model size/compute.
  • Google - "Measuring the Environmental Impact of Delivering AI at Google Scale" (Aug 2025) arXiv:2508.15734 - Google's own production-measured figures for a median Gemini Apps text prompt: 0.24 Wh energy, 0.03 gCO2e, 0.26 mL water; reports a 44x reduction in per-prompt carbon over the prior 12 months.

Note: Anthropic has not published a per-query or training-level carbon/energy methodology for Claude as of mid-2026. Where this dashboard estimates Claude's footprint, it is explicitly flagged as an extrapolation from comparable models, not first-party data.

Water Consumption

  • Li, Yang, Islam, Ren - "Making AI Less 'Thirsty': Uncovering and Addressing the Secret Water Footprint of AI Models" (2023) arXiv:2304.03271 (also in Communications of the ACM) - GPT-3 training consumed an estimated ~700,000 L on-site and ~5.4 million L total; inference estimated at roughly 10-25 mL per prompt (2023-era infrastructure).
  • Jegham et al., "How Hungry is AI?" (2025) arXiv:2505.09598 - water-per-prompt reported across 30 commercial models with more current infrastructure.
  • Google - "Measuring the Environmental Impact of Delivering AI at Google Scale" (2025) arXiv:2508.15734 - Gemini-specific measured figure of 0.26 mL water per median text prompt.
  • World Economic Forum - "The Water Challenge for Semiconductor Manufacturing and Big Tech" weforum.org, 2024
  • IDTechEx - "Water Usage in Semiconductor Manufacturing to Double by 2035" idtechex.com - a single advanced fab can use 20-38 million L/day; TSMC alone used 101 billion L in 2023.
  • Rare - "AI & Water Use: Context, Scale, and Justice" (2026) rare.org - context and equity framing around AI-related water use.

Electricity Consumption

  • Google - "Measuring the Environmental Impact of Delivering AI at Google Scale" (2025) arXiv:2508.15734 - Gemini: 0.24 Wh per median text prompt (May 2025), a full-stack measurement including host CPUs, RAM, and data center overhead.
  • Epoch AI - "How much energy does ChatGPT use?" (2025) epoch.ai - independent estimate that a typical GPT-4o query uses ~0.3 Wh.
  • Patterson et al. (2021) arXiv:2104.10350 - training-side electricity; GPT-3's training run used an estimated 1,287 MWh.
  • Hannah Ritchie (Our World in Data) - "How much electricity does AI consume?" (2025) hannahritchie.substack.com - sourced synthesis and broader grid/data-center context.

Note: OpenAI's only public electricity figure (0.34 Wh) comes from a statement by Sam Altman, not a technical report, so this dashboard uses the independently-derived Epoch AI figure instead. Anthropic has disclosed no per-query figures at all as of mid-2026.

Metals and Minerals Consumption

  • Berg, Ziemer, Polo Anaya (CSIS) - "Mineral Demands for Resilient Semiconductor Supply Chains: The Role of the Western Hemisphere" (2025) csis.org - flags gallium, germanium, palladium, and silicon as the minerals most central to chip production.
  • "Chipping in: Critical minerals for semiconductor manufacturing in the U.S." (MIT Science Policy Review, 2023) sciencepolicyreview.org - technical breakdown of which minerals go into which parts of chip fabrication.
  • Foreign Policy Analytics - "Artificial Intelligence and the Critical Minerals Crunch" (2025) fpanalytics.foreignpolicy.com - framing of AI-driven GPU demand and mineral supply concentration.

Note: no public bill-of-materials data exists per GPU or AI chip from NVIDIA, TSMC, or the AI companies themselves, so this dashboard does not fabricate a per-prompt minerals estimate when EcoLogits itself doesn't report one.
